## The one-line thesis

We take care of the overall wealth of our clients by understanding their professional and personal situation, and customise a portfolio that can generate superior long-term returns by selecting best-performing funds, reduce risk by diversifying across asset classes, allocate strategically to meet goals, and rebalance to take advantage of market imbalances and cash-flow needs. Our dynamic portfolios protect what you cannot afford to lose, grow what you can afford to invest, and stay inside guardrails for your long-term prosperity — without being held hostage to human biases. Your portfolio is an extension of your life, personality, and goals, and our allocation approach is built to ensure alignment.

## Five (well, seven) principles that drive every recommendation

| Principle | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Alignment with your horizon** | You may need money in the near term or for long-term wealth creation. We ring-fence known short-term needs before any market exposure is decided. We have seen many cases where an asset–liability or investment–cashflow mismatch causes permanent loss of capital — for example, selling equity when cash is needed urgently but markets are down. We avoid that scenario by construction. |
| **2. Blend your risk capacity and appetite** | Our approach blends your willingness to take risk with your ability to take risk. We assess what you can afford to risk from your age, income and expenses, debt and property, and combine it with our read of your experience and psychology. When the two diverge sharply, we flag it rather than over-fit to either. The result is a customised portfolio, not a standard product. |
| **3. Allocate according to your goals** | Goals vary by life stage and need — wealth creation, regular income, children's education, buying a house — and by *when* each must be met. We lean on less volatile assets for near-term goals and a fully diversified mix for long-term life goals. The blend can include large-, mid-, small-cap and international equities, value and sector funds, dynamic-duration bonds, arbitrage funds, and gold/silver. You get full visibility into how the portfolio is positioned for each goal. |
| **4. Superior returns with the right selection** | We select funds by evaluating thousands of candidates against past returns, consistency, drawdowns, portfolio quality, manager longevity, fund-house reputation and more — over a long history. This allows selection without human bias, targeting better post-tax returns than the market for equity and better than fixed deposits for debt. Where active management does not add value, we use passive funds for their lower cost. |
| **5. Dynamic management with contrarian calls on market cycles** | Markets move through up- and down-cycles. The approach is built to lean against extremes — guarding against excessive froth and leaning in when valuations cheapen — through a market view that nudges allocations within their bands. We are not timing markets for short-term gains; we are protecting the portfolio at extremes. The market-view setting the plan uses is neutral on every asset class and style, so plans sit near the middle of their bands, and market moves reach your portfolio through rebalancing back to target. |
| **6. Tax-efficient by default** | Tax outcomes are built into the allocation, not bolted on. We favour fund structures that defer or reduce tax drag for higher-tax clients, and we lift post-tax returns on the fixed-income sleeve where the tax treatment allows. We weigh time horizon and the tax impact of selling at that horizon when picking funds. |
| **7. Guardrails over guesses** | Every allocation we recommend lives inside guardrails, with bands for every asset class and subgroup; market views nudge within a band, never breach it, so no single view produces a wild outlier portfolio. A preference you save yourself is honoured as you set it, even outside those bands, and applies only to the holdings-aware plan (see Practical_Asset_Allocation.md). |

We are a holistic wealth advisor — not just a goal-based tool, not just a risk-assessment tool, not just a fund-comparison platform, and not just a tax-advising tool.

## How a recommendation is built — at a high level

Every Ask PI recommendation is the output of three deliberate, auditable steps. We can walk through the reasoning behind any of them on demand.

The plan allocates your investable corpus: the holdings in your linked portfolio (your mutual funds, plus any linked bank-account balances), or the investable savings on your profile where those are larger. Shares you hold directly sit outside it — they still appear in your portfolio view and count in your cashflow projections, but no plan targets, buys or sells them. Loans are not deducted from the corpus either; they shape the plan through your risk score (Step 1).

### Step 1 — Score risk capacity and risk willingness

We form a view on your *ability* to take risk (from your age, income and expenses, debt and property) and your *willingness* to take it (from onboarding), then combine them — with willingness leading and capacity anchoring. Where the two diverge meaningfully, we flag it for a conversation rather than silently splitting the difference. The result is the single risk score that drives the rest of the build. The full reasoning lives in `Risk_Profiling.md`.

### Step 2 — Carve-outs for short-term needs: protect the near term first

- **Emergency fund (off by default):** your default plan does NOT carve out an emergency reserve — it puts your full corpus to work against your goals. The approach supports reserving a few months of household expenses in liquid, low-volatility funds ahead of every other bucket, and you can see that effect on your plan as a what-if in chat, unless you have saved investment preferences: a saved preference suspends the emergency reserve and near-term carve-outs in the plan chat shows (see Practical_Asset_Allocation.md).
- **Short-term goals:** near-term outflows are kept entirely in fixed income so the money is available on demand. Each goal is valued at what it will cost on its date, inflation included, and goals are funded nearest first from three sources, in order:
  1. **Debt and arbitrage funds you already hold.**
  2. **Your monthly SIP, front-loaded** — all of it counts toward the nearest goal not yet covered until that goal is covered, then toward the next, rather than being split across goals.
  3. **Your existing savings** — set aside in fixed income now, but only for the part your SIP cannot build up by each goal's date.

  We assume none of this money earns a return; any growth simply counts the next time your plan is worked out. If your savings cannot cover what is left, we report the shortfall rather than hide it, and any SIP your goals don't need goes to your long-term plan. This ideal plan assumes you hold nothing yet, so it starts from the SIP you have told us about; the plan built on the portfolio you actually own starts from the debt and arbitrage funds you hold (see `Practical_Asset_Allocation.md`). We favour the most tax-efficient fixed-income structure for your tax situation, leaning toward arbitrage-style funds for higher-tax clients and plain debt otherwise.

- **Two horizons:** every goal is either near-term — due within roughly the next couple of years, measured from the end of the current financial year — or long-term, with no in-between bucket. A goal beyond the near-term line is planned with the long-term portfolio below, where time is on its side.

**Why this matters:** any short-term need must sit in less volatile investments. Equity and commodities are volatile in the short run but more predictable over the long run. Match short-term goals with short-duration assets, and long-term goals with long-term investments. One of the most common reasons long-term portfolios fail is a forced sale at the wrong time — carve-outs make that structurally unlikely.

### Step 3 — Long-term allocation: asset class → subgroup → guardrails

#### Step 3a — Asset-class allocation

The remaining corpus is allocated across **equities**, **debt** and **others** based on your risk profile. Conservative profiles cap equity tightly; aggressive profiles raise the equity ceiling and, at the very top of the scale, can take the debt floor very low.

- **Intergenerational-transfer view:** when a long-term goal is explicitly about passing wealth to the next generation — and the holder is at a life stage where the heir's horizon genuinely dominates — the approach lifts the growth posture to reflect the longer effective horizon. This view is not switched on in the product, so the standard long-term posture applies to every client.
- **Market-view overlay:** the approach lets a house view on the relative attractiveness of asset classes and subgroups nudge allocations *within* the relevant bands; views never breach the bands. The market-view setting the plan uses is neutral on every asset class and style, so your mix sits near the middle of the band for your risk profile rather than leaning toward either end.

#### Step 3b — Subgroup allocation: where exactly the money goes

- **Diversified core:** a tax-efficient, equity-tilted multi-asset fund anchors the long-term equity, sized to do real diversification work without dominating. It is preferred for its tax efficiency, its built-in diversification, and its lower churn.
- **Growth styles:** the equity left after the core is split across low-, mid- and high-beta funds for stability through to growth, and US equities for global exposure, inside per-style bands keyed to your risk profile. Value funds (contrarian bets at valuation extremes) and sector funds (emerging structural themes) enter only when we hold a strong conviction in them; at the plan's neutral market-view setting they are left out. Styles with no room at your risk level — such as high-beta funds for the most conservative profiles — are left out too, and very small slivers are consolidated so you hold meaningful positions rather than a long tail.
- **Debt:** long-term debt favours the most tax-efficient structure for your situation.
- **Others:** the residual goes to gold and commodities. For higher-risk profiles — the upper end of Moderately Aggressive as well as Aggressive — the separate gold sleeve is dropped unless we hold a clearly positive view on commodities, and commodity exposure comes only through the multi-asset core.

#### Step 3c — Guardrails: validation before delivery

- Class totals reconcile to 100%, and that reconciliation is enforced — a plan that doesn't sum correctly is never shown to you; it fails safely instead.
- Every market-view nudge is clamped inside its band as it is computed, by construction, so a band breach shouldn't occur; a final check confirms every subgroup sits inside its band and every position rolls up to its parent class, and any anomaly is flagged in our system logs.
- Percentages are presented clean and round. The rupee amounts in your plan are worked out to the rupee, so the parts always add up to exactly what you have — nothing is lost to rounding along the way. The amounts you actually invest or move — SIPs, lump sums and switches — are rounded to clean ₹100 figures, and the monthly amount set aside for a near-term goal is rounded up, never down, so rounding never leaves a goal short.

## Exploring and changing your plan in chat

- **What-if questions are live but hypothetical.** Ask "what if my risk score were 7?", "what if I add ₹50 lakh?", or "what if I kept an emergency reserve?" and the full plan recomputes on the spot for comparison — but the result is never saved. A what-if on your income or tax regime alone leaves the allocation unchanged: the plan reads the risk score and income-tax rate already on your profile, and a what-if does not re-score them.
- **No corpus, no plan.** If we have no investable amount on file, chat asks you to upload your CAMS statement or start a monthly SIP rather than show an empty plan.
- **Chat does not save changes.** Saying "save this" or "lock it in" in chat does not persist anything. To change your plan durably, update your Profile — the plan regenerates from the updated inputs.
- **Investment preferences are set on their own page, not in chat.** If you tell chat you want more equity or fewer mid caps, it will point you to your preferences page rather than rebuilding the plan on the spot — a standing instruction deserves a deliberate save, not a passing remark. Once saved, the preference shapes the holdings-aware plan and the SIP, lump-sum and rebalancing advice built on it; this ideal plan stays preference-free, as a second opinion on your own instructions.
- **The numbers you see are holdings-aware.** Chat shows the practical allocation built on these ideal targets (see `Practical_Asset_Allocation.md`), so the displayed mix reflects the portfolio you actually own, not just the theory.
- **When the plan recomputes.** A fresh plan runs when you start a new conversation or when you explicitly ask for a full redo. If you update your profile, the fresh numbers show up the next time you start a new conversation — a same-session edit won't change the plan already in front of you until then.

## Why a customer should trust this approach

| Question | Our answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Why is my equity at X% and not higher?** | Usually one or more of: (a) a lower risk profile — we can identify which input (age, income and expenses, debt, property) is driving it; (b) near-term goals being funded first, which need less volatile assets and crowd out equity; (c) where you sit within the band for your risk profile — with our market-view setting neutral, that is near the middle of the band, not the top. |
| **Why so much in liquid / short debt?** | Same triage: a lower risk profile leaning to debt, or significant near-term goals consuming the corpus — near-term money is kept in fixed income by construction. |
| **Why isn't the full cost of my near-term goal set aside from my savings?** | Because your monthly SIP counts first. Whatever it can build up by the goal's date doesn't need to leave your savings today, so only the part it can't reach in time is set aside in fixed income now — and if even that is more than your savings can cover, we tell you the shortfall. In the plan built on the portfolio you actually own, debt and arbitrage funds you already hold count before the SIP (see `Practical_Asset_Allocation.md`). |
| **Why hybrid (multi-asset) funds in particular?** | Tax efficiency on the debt portion, built-in diversification across equity styles and sometimes international assets and commodities, and lower churn because the manager rebalances within the fund. |
| **Why won't you just chase the hot sector?** | Sector exposure is bounded by design, only ever a modest slice, and enters the plan only when we hold a strong conviction — at the plan's neutral market-view setting, the plan we recommend carries no sector funds. We don't chase hot sectors; when we do allocate to a sector, it is one whose valuations have cheapened or that has genuine structural tailwinds. |
| **What changes when markets change?** | Our market view can nudge allocations toward conviction *within* the bands — never beyond them — and it is set to neutral, so your targets sit near the middle of their bands. Market moves reach your portfolio through rebalancing: when prices pull your holdings far enough from target, rebalancing brings them back. We can always explain why the view is set the way it is. |

## What this thesis is — and is not

This document is a directional reference. It is not a prediction, not a guarantee of returns, and not a substitute for the actual recommendation, which is always personalised. The bands, formulas and overlays behind it are reviewed periodically and may evolve as market structure and tax law change. When that happens, this thesis is updated and dated.
