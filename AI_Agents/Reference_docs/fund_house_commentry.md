# Market Commentary — August 2026

**As of:** August 14, 2026

## Equities

### 1. What is the overall outlook on equities in India? How has the position or outlook changed recently vs the last few years or months? What are the reasons?

Prozpr view: We are positive on equities in the medium to long term after two years of near zero returns in the market. But ruppee weakness has made headline indices cheaper by 15% in dollar terms. Positive business cycle and earnings growth will continue to help investors compound money at 10%+ in equities. Return of FII flows into Indian equities can further help accelerate returns for existing holders.  However, even after valuations reset in the last 2 years, the valuations are not optically cheap as aggregate valuations are still higher than historical average (Nifty ~18.59x one-year forward P/E; market-cap-to-GDP ~141%) and small and mid caps are trading at premium with continuous inflows from domestic retail in small and mid cap mutual funds. Thus discipline and staggered investments in higher quality equities and quality managers is better.     

**ICICI Prudential (Aug 2026):** Constructive with a staggered-entry approach. Their "V-C-S" framework shows Valuations have "re-set" (the froth of the last two years has come off; India's 1-year USD return of roughly -7% is among the weakest globally), the business Cycle "holds firm" (credit growth 18.6% YoY, capacity utilisation 77.4% vs 75% average), and Sentiments are neutral (FIIs turned buyers in July). This is an explicit change from September 2024, when they were "overall cautious on equities" at the market peak — they have since raised net equity across their asset-allocation schemes (e.g., Balanced Advantage Fund from 31.8% in Sep-24 to 68.4% in Jul-26). They recommend investing in a staggered manner via SIP/STP and continue to prefer hybrid/asset-allocation schemes because near-term volatility from the geopolitical environment is "inevitable."

**Canara Robeco (Aug 2026):** Constructive on Indian equities with a 3–5 year view. India is in a business cycle / credit growth / earnings cycle through FY26–28E. FY25 was a cyclical slowdown whose drivers have largely reversed; earnings growth moderated to mid-high single digits in FY25/26 but is expected to normalise to low-to-mid double digits in FY27E/28E, supported by government capex and consumption boosts (GST cuts, income-tax cuts and state welfare schemes adding 1.5–2% of GDP), rate cuts and liquidity, a good monsoon and rural real wages. Nifty at 19x FY27E / 17.5x FY28E is described as a "cheap valuation zone from a medium-term perspective." The key caveat: a war extending beyond 1–2 months would create downside risk to earnings and markets via energy prices.

**HDFC (Jul 2026):** Optimistic over the medium-to-long term, citing key trade deals, an attractive domestic growth outlook, healthy corporate profitability and pro-growth policies (income tax and GST relief). Near-term risks are a flare-up in geopolitical tensions and cyclical moderation in corporate earnings. They note aggregate valuations are higher than historical average (Nifty ~18.59x one-year forward P/E; market-cap-to-GDP ~141%), which "increases the importance of stock selection."

**PPFAS (Jul 2026):** Sanguine and improving. CIO Rajeev Thakkar writes that the roughly two-year rangebound market is "a feature and not a bug" of equity investing, and is not noteworthy for duration or magnitude. Having been cautious during the 2024 exuberance (cash peaked at ~25% of Flexi Cap), they now say "the available opportunities are increasing, and the outlook for future returns here on appears to be improving" — cash is down to ~14–15% and they anticipate single-digit cash levels in time.

**CLSA (Aug 2026):** No explicit buy/sell stance, but their proprietary India Bull-Bear sentiment index sits in the neutral zone (49.4% bullish, down from 53.4% a week earlier). The Nifty trades at 18.3x 12-month forward P/E — a 12.6% premium to its long-term average of ~16.3x — and the bond-yield-minus-earnings-yield gap of 1.3ppts is above its one-year average of ~1ppt, suggesting valuations are not cheap on their metrics. Their India Focus portfolio remains fully invested and has outperformed the Nifty by 8.4ppts in CY26-to-date.

**Kotak (Jul 2026):** No explicit market call in these documents. They report Indian equities ended July with modest gains (~2%) after a volatile month marked by crude swings, US-Iran tensions and 1QFY27 results; the Nifty trades at 19.6x FY2027E earnings on their estimates, with Nifty-50 1QFY27 net income coming in 4.7% ahead of their expectations (11.1% YoY growth) across the 39 companies that had reported.

## Bonds

### 1. Which category or kind of fixed income / debt instruments are they recommending to buy, and which are they cautious on? What are the reasons?

Prozpr view: For fixed incoem portfolio, we recommend investors to invest in (1) Liquid funds or overnight funds when holding period is less than 1 year as these funds provide higher returns than saving banks and can be redeemed in 1 day (2) Arbitrage funds for holding between 1-2 years as these funds are taxed at only 12.5% vs individual marginal tax rates on debt ( better for people with tax rates > 20%) (3) Income plus Arbitrage funds for holding more than 2 years as these funds are taxed at only 12.5% vs individual marginal tax rates on debt or deposit ( better for people with tax rates > 20%) and these funds can manage allocations betweeb debt funds and arbitrage funds dynamically depending on where the returns are more attractive.  

**ICICI Prudential (Aug 2026):** Buy the **short end of the corporate bond curve**; recommended categories are **floating interest, short term, corporate bond, and banking & PSU debt** funds (with All Seasons Bond Fund as a tactical/active-duration option and Income plus Arbitrage FOF). They are **cautious on aggressive long-term duration bets** — "much of the easing in yields may already be behind us." Their proprietary Debt Valuation Index reads 3.82 ("cautious" zone), supporting an "Accruals + Active Duration Management" approach: short-dated corporate bonds for the core, tactical exposure to long-dated SDLs/G-secs only. Reasons: robust credit growth narrowing the output gap and raising inflation concerns, offset by good liquidity (FCNR(B) boom) and possibly narrower risk premiums.
